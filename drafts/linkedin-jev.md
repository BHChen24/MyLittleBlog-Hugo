# LinkedIn draft: Jev

A support app can spend an entire LLM call answering “where should this message go?” That made Jev interesting to me.

TypeSafe released Jev in early access on September 15. Give it text and a question with defined answers, and it returns a choice, a score, or a yes/no probability. The app decides what to do with that result.

If I were building a support inbox, I'd have Jev sort messages as they arrive. For example: “I was charged twice and need this fixed today.” Jev could identify it as a billing issue and flag the urgency. The app could then check the transaction and decide whether an LLM or a person should handle the response.

TypeSafe reports fast responses, though I haven't used Jev myself yet. A result can fit the required format and still be wrong, so decision quality matters as much as speed. Open alternatives such as Laya are also appearing.

The bigger question for TypeSafe is whether developers will keep choosing Jev as open alternatives improve. That could support a business of its own, or make Jev valuable to a larger AI company.
